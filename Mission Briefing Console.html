Quick start
  • Open the Images tab and load a Portrait A (and Portrait B for a second speaker), some Logo / images, and optionally an Extra window picture. You can also drag image files straight onto a box.
  • Press Voice file and choose your recording, then press Play (Space). The oscilloscope draws the live waveform and the portrait glows when the voice is loud.
  • Click a box that holds several images to step to the next one.
  • Not sure what a part does? Open Help › Samples to load sample pictures, a sample voice and a ready-made radar scene, and try each part with one click.
  • Press Export video to record the whole console plus the voice into one video file.
  • Press H (or the ☰ button on a phone) to hide the controls while presenting.
The five boxes
  • Box 1 – Speaker portrait. Switches between Portrait A and B. Glows with the voice.
  • Box 2 – Logo / images. Faction logo or mission pictures. Can be shaped (circle, shield, triangle, hexagon) and can spin like a coin.
  • Box 3 – Oscilloscope. Always a plain rectangle. Shrinks into a strip when the radar is open.
  • Box 4 – Extra window. Optional picture window above the scope (Show fourth box).
  • Box 5 – Radar. A digital grid with contacts that move on programmed courses (Show radar box (5th)).

Boxes 1, 2, 4 and 5 can each be shown or hidden with the buttons in the top row. They grow into view and shrink away with a short animation, and the video records it. The remaining boxes resize to fill the space.

3D view of the radar terrain
  • In the Radar tab, open 3D view and turn it on. By default the 3D ground is built from the hills, peaks, ridges and basins you placed with the Terrain row, and your placed urban areas and roads appear as buildings and streets. You can switch the source to procedural terrain (seed, roughness, relief, sea level) or a grayscale heightmap image.
  • Markers can be placed and moved top-down over a hill-shaded map, or directly on the tilted terrain. Preview motion, playback and export always show the 3D view with markers standing on the terrain.
  • Turn on Edit in 3D view to place, select and drag markers (including static position markers such as buildings) on the tilted terrain. Clicks land on the terrain surface under the pointer and still snap to the grid.
  • Open Urban areas & roads to paint built-up areas (drawn as buildings in the 3D view) and to draw roads that follow the terrain. They appear in the top-down view and in the exported video.
  • The Altitude slider on a selected contact (0 to 300) raises it above the terrain in the 3D view, with a stem and ground ring. It shows as ALT in the label in both views.
  • For a contact that follows a path, use Altitude for in the path controls: New points sets the height of waypoints you add next, or pick a numbered waypoint to change it. Each waypoint can have its own altitude and speed (leave speed blank to keep the current speed); contacts climb, descend and speed up or slow down smoothly on the way to it. Speeds up on a moving contact adds steady acceleration (negative slows it down).
  • Tilt, rotate and auto-rotate set the camera. Terrain on/off is recorded by Record cues.
News ticker and video in box 4
  • Show the fourth box, then load images, GIFs or video (mp4/webm) into it from the Ticker tab or the Images tab. Click the box to step through them.
  • Turn the ticker on in the Ticker tab or press Y. Type one headline per line; they scroll in a loop along the top or bottom of box 4.
  • The ticker appears in the exported video, freezes on Pause, and on/off is recorded by Record cues.
  • Video in box 4 is silent and does not use the coin spin.
Playing, pausing and exporting video
  • Play / Stop runs the voice file. Pause freezes the voice, the video recording and the radar together; make any changes you like while paused, then press Resume and they appear in the video straight away.
  • Export video starts a recording that includes the voice, then saves a video file when the voice ends. Keep this browser tab in view while exporting, or the recording freezes.
  • With Boxes open at start of recording on (Look tab, or O), the video opens on an empty screen and every box grows into view one after another before the voice starts. Turn it off for a plain start.
  • With Boxes wink out at end of recording on (Look tab, or Q), the voice ends and radio interference builds across the screen, then each window collapses to a bright line and winks out in turn, with a burst of static in the audio (Static sound at the end turns the sound off).
  • Export frame chooses the video shape: match screen uses your window's proportions, 16:9 uses a fixed widescreen frame.
Conversation tab: two speakers, auto-switch and cues
  • Give each speaker a name; the name shows on the portrait.
  • Switch speakers with 1, 2, S, the Speaker button, an Auto switch timer (every N seconds), or by recording cues.
  • Record cues: turn it on, press Play, then do things at the right moments. It records speaker switches, showing or hiding boxes 1, 2, 4 and 5, spins, and image steps. Turn recording off and play again, and everything replays at the same times (including in an exported video). Clear cues erases them.
  • Each recording remembers how things looked when you pressed Play, so the replay starts from the same state.
Portrait tab
  • Backdrop color / Backdrop image sit behind a transparent portrait.
  • Clean portrait background removes a baked-in checkerboard pattern; Strength tunes how aggressively.
  • Speaker name › Text size makes the name on the portrait bigger or smaller (also [ and ]). It applies to the exported video too, and a long name shrinks to fit the box.
  • Zoom, Left/right, Up/down frame the portrait. You can also drag the portrait in the box and use the mouse wheel to zoom. Reset position puts it back.
Logo & spin tab
  • Spin box 2 / Spin box 4 turn the logo like a 3D coin (box 4 spins as a slab).
  • Shape box 2 turns the shaping on or off. Choose Circle, Shield, Triangle or Hexagon badge.
  • Crop to fill fills the whole shape (with zoom and position sliders); Fit whole image shows the entire picture on a Fill color.
Look tab: CRT effects and colors
  • CRT lines adds scanlines (adjust Strength); CRT bevel adds the monitor frame to boxes 1, 2, 4 and 5.
  • Theme: Hue, Scope color and Background color. Reset colors returns to default.
  • Presets: four built in (Green, Amber terminal, Blue radar, Red alert). Type a name and press Save preset to keep your own; they are stored in this browser. Your last colors are remembered.
Radar: grid and controls
  • Turn the radar on with Show radar box (5th); the Radar tab appears.
  • Terrain draws topographic contour lines under the grid, built from pieces you place. Set Place › Type to Terrain piece, choose a Piece (Hill, Peak, Ridge or Basin, a dip or lake) and click the radar. While Terrain piece is the chosen type, white dots mark each piece: drag a dot to move a piece, or click it to resize it, change its Height, rotate or lengthen a ridge, or give it a name. Contours redraw instantly, every fourth line is thicker with an elevation label, and each hill or peak gets a spot height. Press Delete to remove the selected piece.
  • Urban terrain is optional. Choose Urban area or Road as the Piece and click the radar. An urban area is a district of streets and buildings: set its Size, Density (how built-up) and Angle (to turn the street grid), and give it a name like a town. A road is a straight route with an Angle and Length; chain several to make a route. They don't change the ground height, have their own Urban color, and the Urban areas switch (or U) hides them all without deleting them.
  • Fortifications are also optional pieces: Fort (a walled compound with corner bastions, a gate and a keep), Bunker (a hardened block with a firing slit and a dashed field of fire), Trench line, Barbed wire, Tank traps (a row of dragon's teeth) and Minefield (a dashed area scattered with crosses). Turn a piece with Angle (the gate and the bunker's slit face the way it points), stretch lines with Length, and name any of them. Like roads they don't change the ground height. They have their own Fortification color, and the Fortifications switch (or K) hides them all without deleting them.
  • The Terrain row also has Starting layout and Scatter new terrain (N), which drop a ready-made landscape you can then edit piece by piece, Clear terrain, Contour lines, Roughness (how irregular the pieces' edges are), its own Terrain color, and switches for relief shading, elevation labels and spot heights. M turns terrain on or off. It is part of the recording.
  • Grid color is separate from the main theme. Grid size sets how many columns (more columns = smaller squares). Sweep shows the rotating sweep line. Snap to grid drops markers into the center of a square.
  • Each square has a reference like F7, shown under every marker's label.
  • Every contact has its own Rotation slider (0 to 359 degrees) in its Selected panel. While Auto is ticked it faces the way it moves; move the slider or untick Auto to point it any direction you like. Shapes without a front (squares, stars and so on) turn too, in both views.
  • Contact shapes include Fighter aircraft, two Cargo planes (jet and turboprop), Gun turret and Missile turret, Dropship, Mech and Tank. Aircraft and tanks turn to face the direction they are moving.
  • A combat marker's Altitude lifts it off the ground in the 3D view. Tick Only hits contacts near this altitude and it only affects contacts flying within about 50 of that height.
  • To close the Selected panel, click an empty spot on the radar, press Esc, or press Done. While you are adding waypoints, clicks still add waypoints.
  • Undo and Redo (buttons beside Reset contacts, keys Z and Shift+Z, or Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y) step back and forward through your radar edits: placing, moving, deleting and changing contacts, combat markers and terrain pieces. A drag or a slider move counts as one step. They work while the radar is editable (not during playback). Colors and the grid are not part of the history.
  • Motion speed speeds up or slows down all movement (0.25× to 6×). Preview motion runs the radar without audio. During playback and export, contacts move with the voice. Reset contacts returns everyone to their starting spots, Clear all removes everything.
  • Your radar setup (contacts, markers and settings) is saved in this browser.
Radar: placing contacts and static positions
  • Set Place → Type, Faction (Friendly blue, Hostile red, Unknown amber) and Shape, optionally type a Label for next marker, then click or tap the radar. Drag a marker to move it; click it to edit it.
  • Moving contact: choose how it moves in the Selected row: Holds still, Straight line (set Heading in degrees, 0 = up, and Speed in cells per second; at the edge it can bounce, wrap or stop) or Follows path (turn on Add waypoints and click the grid; tick Loop to repeat).
  • Appears at makes a contact enter the radar at a chosen second of the recording.
  • Turrets (gun and missile) turn to face the contact they Attack. A gun turret's attack arrow shows rapid tracers, and a missile turret's shows a missile with a trail. Attacks are visual by default: set Attack effect to destroy the target once it has been inside Range (grid cells) for the Fire time. A bolt strikes the target and its arrow only shows while it is in range.
  • Attacks (in the Selected panel) draws an animated arrow from the selected contact to another contact, in both views. It follows both as they move and disappears if either is not yet shown or has been destroyed. For a contact on a path, Shown limits the arrow to the time it is heading to a chosen waypoint (dimmed in the editor when that waypoint isn't the first).
  • View (in the Preview row, next to Play) swaps between the overhead view and the 3D view at any time, including while the preview is running. It is the same switch as 3D view in the 3D section.
  • Loop (next to Play) restarts the preview once every moving contact has finished its path or been destroyed, after a short pause. Max restarts it anyway after that many seconds, for contacts that never finish, such as looping paths or contacts that wrap round the edges.
  • Formation (Selected panel) makes a contact fly with a leader: it keeps its starting offset from the leader and turns with it, so a wing moves as one. Followers ignore their own movement and altitude path (they keep their height difference), and break off if the leader is destroyed. Dotted lines show the links in the editor.
  • Camera follows (or Follow in the 3D camera row) makes the 3D view centre on a contact and zoom in; Follow zoom sets how close.
  • When a combat marker goes off, a strike bolt flies from the marker to each contact it affects, ending in an impact ring.
  • Static position is a large fixed icon with a centered label, for bases, objectives and landmarks. It never moves. Size resizes any marker.
  • Shapes include circle, square, diamond, triangle, star, cross, hexagon, crosshair, base/building and flag.
Radar: combat markers
  • Place one with Type → Combat marker. By default it is hidden during the show and pops up (a ring and flash) when a contact crosses it. While editing you see it as a ghosted starburst with a dashed zone.
  • Radius sets the size of the zone. Shows chooses hidden until triggered or always visible.
  • Triggered by limits which faction can set it off (any, hostile, friendly or unknown).
  • Affects: everything inside the zone (including contacts sitting still there) or only the contact that crosses.
  • Contact decides the outcome: becomes a kill mark (an X is left behind) or disappears.
  • For each contact, At combat markers can be Is hit (default), Survives (the marker still pops but this contact lives) or Ignores them (passes through without triggering).
  • Contacts that are not moving never set a marker off, and static positions are never affected. Every run starts fresh with all markers hidden again.
On a phone or tablet
  • Tap to place, drag to move, tap a marker to edit it. The ☰ button (top right) hides or shows the controls; with controls hidden, taps on the radar do not add markers.
  • Swipe the button row and the tab row sideways to reach everything. The panel under the tabs scrolls.
  • The grid defaults to a chunkier size on small screens; change it with Grid size.
Keyboard shortcuts

Shortcuts are ignored while you are typing in a text box and when Ctrl, Cmd or Alt is held.

Good to know
  • Presets, last colors and the radar setup live in this browser, so they stay on this computer and browser.
  • Keep the browser tab in view while exporting video.
  • The patch lettering uses Impact if your computer has it.

Samples load generated pictures, a synthetic voice and a ready-made radar scene so you can see what each part does without any files of your own. Loading a sample replaces what is currently in that box.

Start here

Loads sample portraits, logos, extra pictures, a voice and a radar scene in one go, then opens the radar. Press Play afterward to see everything respond to the voice.

Box 1 · Speaker portrait

Shows who is speaking and glows brighter when the voice is loud. Two portraits make a conversation; switch with 1, 2 or S, an auto timer, or recorded cues.

Box 2 · Logo and mission images

A faction logo or a set of pictures. Click the box to step through them. It can be a circle, shield, triangle or hexagon, and can spin like a coin.

Box 3 · Oscilloscope and the voice

Always visible. Draws the live waveform of the voice file and drives the portrait glow. The sample voice is synthetic speech-like audio, with pauses, so you can see the scope respond.

Box 4 · Extra window

An optional picture window above the scope, for a map, photo or document. It opens and closes with a short animation that is also recorded.

Hiding boxes 1 and 2

Hide the portrait or the logo to give the other boxes more room. They shrink away and grow back, and the layout fills the gap.

Box 5 · Radar

A digital grid with contacts that move on programmed courses. The sample scene contains:

  • FOB ALPHA (friendly base) and ENEMY HQ (hostile flag): static positions with big labels.
  • PATROL: a friendly contact looping round a path.
  • RAIDER, WINGMAN, SCOUT: three contacts heading through a hidden combat marker (below).
  • DRIFTER: flies into a visible kill zone and vanishes. SCOUT only appears 3 seconds in.
  • Terrain, all placed by hand: a hill under the base, NORTH RIDGE and the peak THE ANVIL forming the pass where the ambush waits, high ground behind ENEMY HQ, a LAKE basin, and optional urban terrain: the town of KARSK and ROUTE 9.

Terrain · topographic map

The sample radar scene already has terrain you can edit (Edit its terrain on the Radar card). Contour lines under the radar grid, made from pieces you place: hills (round rings), peaks (tight, steep rings), ridges (long ovals you can rotate) and basins (dips or lakes). Optional urban terrain adds urban areas (a district of streets and buildings with adjustable density) and roads, in their own color, and switches off with Urban areas. Move, resize or delete any piece and the map redraws at once. Try it: choose Place › Type › Terrain piece and click the radar.

Combat markers

In the sample scene the hidden AMBUSH marker sits in the pass between NORTH RIDGE and THE ANVIL, which is why the contacts funnel through it. When you run it: RAIDER is hit and becomes a kill mark, WINGMAN is set to Survives so it passes through (the marker still pops), and SCOUT is set to Ignores them. While editing, hidden markers show as dashed zones.

Air wing · attacks, formations, altitude, camera

A separate demo scene, best seen in the 3D view (the demo turns it on for you).

  • CARGO LEAD (jet cargo plane) flies a path at altitude 120. ESCORT 1, ESCORT 2 and CARGO 2 (turboprop) are set to Formation, so they hold their offsets and turn with it. Select one to see the dotted link.
  • BANDIT has an Attacks arrow aimed at CARGO LEAD, shown only while heading to waypoint 1. It then flies into the FLAK marker, which is set to the same altitude, and a strike bolt plays.
  • GROUND FIRE sits on the transports' route but is set to altitude 0 and only hits contacts near this altitude, so the planes fly safely over it.
  • SAM SITE (missile turret) is set to Destroys target on ESCORT 2: once the escort has been inside its range for 3 seconds it is shot down and the rest of the formation carries on. AA GUN (gun turret) fires tracers at CARGO LEAD but is set to Visual only.
  • Run with camera following makes the 3D camera track CARGO LEAD.

Look · CRT effects and colors

CRT lines and bevel give the monitor feel; the theme recolors the scope and panels. Try the built-in looks, then restore yours.

Opening and ending of a recording

Exporting video opens with the boxes growing in one after another, and ends with radio static and each window winking out. Preview both without recording.

Cues, pause and export

To build a timed show: turn on Record cues, press Play, then swap speakers and open or hide boxes at the right moments. Turn recording off and play again to replay them. Pause freezes the voice and the video so you can change things, then resume. Everything is captured by Export video.
